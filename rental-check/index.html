        How to verify

        Look up who really owns the property

        Every county keeps public property records. They're usually free to search online. A scammer can copy photos, but they can't change who the county says owns the house.

        1. Find the county. Search the full street address on any map site to see which county it's in.
        2. Open the county assessor's property search. Depending on the state it may be called the assessor, property appraiser, appraisal district, auditor, or tax collector. Use the official government site. Skip paid "property records" sites.
        3. Search by address and note the owner's name and the last sale date. If the house sold last month but the "landlord" says they've owned it for years, ask why.
        4. Optional: check the recorder or register of deeds for the latest deed if the assessor's data looks out of date.
        5. If the owner is a company (LLC), look it up on your state's business registry (usually the Secretary of State) and ask the landlord how they're connected. A real property manager can show a management agreement and has a business you can call.
        6. Compare. The name should match the person you're dealing with, or a management company you've confirmed separately. Ask for the owner to confirm by phone using a number you found yourself, not one from the listing.

        How to verify

        Check whether the photos were copied

        The FTC says scammers often copy a real listing, sometimes one that's for sale rather than for rent, and swap in their own contact details.

        1. Save two or three of the listing photos. On a phone, press and hold the photo and choose Save. On a computer, right-click and save.
        2. Run a reverse image search. Use Google Images (click the camera icon) or Google Lens in the Google app, TinEye, or Bing Visual Search. Upload each photo.
        3. Search the address and the text. Search the full address, then a distinctive sentence from the description in quotation marks.
        4. Read the results. Warning signs: the same photos with a different price, a different contact name, or a "for sale" listing. Seeing the same landlord and price on several rental sites is normal.
